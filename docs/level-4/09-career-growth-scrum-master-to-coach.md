# 09 · Career Growth: Scrum Master to Agile Coach/Director

The path from Scrum Master to enterprise/agile coach or director isn't just
"do Scrum Master tasks for more teams." Each step up changes the unit of
impact and the skill that matters most, and treating it as a scope increase
alone is a common reason promoted Scrum Masters struggle in the next role.

## The career ladder and what changes at each level

| Level | Unit of impact | Core skill that matters most |
|---|---|---|
| Scrum Master | One team's process health | Facilitation, servant leadership, removing team-level impediments |
| Senior Scrum Master / multi-team | Two to three teams, cross-team dependencies | Coordination, Scrum-of-Scrums facilitation, stakeholder communication |
| Agile Coach | A program or business unit | Coaching (not directing), organizational-change literacy, influence without authority |
| Director of Agile / Head of Transformation | The whole organization's operating model | Strategic change management, portfolio-level thinking, executive stakeholder management |

## Skills to build deliberately before the next step

| Target role | Skill gap most candidates underestimate |
|---|---|
| Agile Coach | Letting go of "fixing it yourself" — coaching requires tolerating a team's slower, self-discovered path over your own faster answer |
| Director/Head of Transformation | Speaking in business/financial terms to executives, not process terms; budget and portfolio literacy |
| Either | Data literacy — using metrics (Module 06's outcome metrics) to make a credible case, not just anecdote |

## Building a portfolio of evidence

| Evidence type | Why it matters for promotion/hiring |
|---|---|
| A documented before/after on a real team (metrics + narrative) | Shows outcome ownership, not just activity |
| A framework or practice you introduced that spread beyond your own team | Shows influence without authority, the core coach/director skill |
| A failure you can describe honestly, with what you learned | Signals the self-awareness that's hard to fake at senior levels |
| Certifications (CSM, CSP, SAFe, ICF-aligned coaching credentials) | Table stakes for external hiring at coach/director level; rarely sufficient alone |

## A worked example

A Scrum Master who has run one team well for two years wants to become an
enterprise agile coach. Their instinct is to add more certifications and
wait to be asked. Using this module's ladder, the gap isn't credentials —
it's that they've never influenced anyone outside their own team, and their
proof points are all "I fixed this for my team" rather than "I helped this
spread beyond my team."

They deliberately build the missing evidence: they volunteer to run a
cross-team Scrum of Scrums for two additional teams (building the
coordination skill for the next rung), document a specific practice — a
Definition-of-Ready checklist — that two other teams adopt after seeing it
work, and start speaking in outcome metrics (cycle time, not story points)
when presenting to their own manager. A year later, they have a portfolio
showing influence beyond one team, which is precisely the gap the coach
role tests for — and the certification, pursued afterward, complements
rather than substitutes for that evidence.

## Exercise

You are a Scrum Master for one team and want to move toward an Agile Coach
role within 18 months. Using this module's framework: (1) name the single
biggest skill gap between your current level and Agile Coach, using the
ladder table, (2) design one concrete opportunity in your current role to
practice that skill before you have the title, (3) name one piece of
evidence (from the evidence table) you don't currently have and how you'd
start building it this quarter, and (4) explain in your own words why more
certifications alone wouldn't close the gap you named in (1).
